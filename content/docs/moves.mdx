A <Term id="move">move</Term> is a named piece of motion that you copy into your <Term id="page">page</Term>. Each move is one markdown file in `prompts/moves/` with a snippet of 10 to 40 lines, a note on when to use it, a `Sound:` line and a one-second clip. The library has 130 moves in seven groups.

## Why they exist

A model that writes motion from memory writes the same few moves: a fade up, a scale in, a slide. A move gives the page a proven version instead. Its numbers were tuned against a real clip, and its curve is an `EASE` name from `core/motion/presets.js`, never a hand-fitted `cubic-bezier()`.

The clip is the first thing to read. Choose from the clip, not from the name.

## Use a move in a page

<Steps>
<Step>

Open the gallery at [vawe.dev/video-components](https://vawe.dev/video-components). Each card plays the clip. Pick the move whose clip shows the job you need.

</Step>
<Step>

Read the move file `prompts/moves/<move>.md`. The first paragraph starts with "Use when" and says what the move is for and what it costs.

</Step>
<Step>

Copy the snippet into the page. Keep its numbers. The snippet delays start at 0, so add the start time of your <Term id="beat">beat</Term> to each delay. Name the beat time once, as a custom property such as `--beat-2: 1.60s`.

</Step>
<Step>

Copy the `Sound:` line too. It names a <Term id="voice">voice</Term> and the second to cue it, or says "none".

</Step>
<Step>

Draft only the seconds you changed. See [Draft](/docs/draft).

```bash
bin/vawe dev films/<name>/page.html --from 2 --to 6
```

</Step>
</Steps>

The demo page that each clip came from is `prompts/moves/demo/<move>.html`. It links `demo/demo.css`, and the snippets name its classes. Copy the classes that you use, or write your own.

### Two rules the snippets already follow

Keep both when you change a snippet.

- An exit keyframe has no `from`. A `from` fills backwards over the entrance and hides it.
- `var()` never goes inside the `animation` shorthand. Write the longhands. Chromium drops the whole declaration otherwise.

<Callout kind="note">
A move animates designed things: type, shapes, UI, illustration and colour fields. A photo may be the content of a move, such as a card or a tile, and the move moves it whole. A move that only works by cutting a captured image into depth layers, or by tracking footage, is a footage technique. It is not part of vawe. Sampling a photo into a designed particle form is in scope. See [Image to particles](/docs/image-to-particles).
</Callout>

## The seven groups

`prompts/moves/README.md` lists every move in seven groups, and a "Pick by job" table with one row per job. The group sizes are below.

| group | moves | the job |
|---|---|---|
| Reveal a title | 25 | Type enters the frame. |
| Change between shots | 38 | The seam between two beats. |
| Point the eye | 7 | One word or one UI part gets attention. |
| Move the camera | 16 | The camera, focus and exposure of a world. |
| End a film | 10 | The last beat. |
| Product moments | 21 | A product does one real thing. |
| Grounds | 13 | A living ground, plus a few loops and photo effects. |

Five moves are featured first in the gallery: `marquee-along-path`, `lens`, `counter-dive`, `ticker-takeover` and `shape-trace-morph`. Three blind reviewers scored every clip, and only clips with a median of 8 or more can be featured.

## From a move to a film

A single move is one second. A film needs a chain. A <Term id="recipe">recipe</Term> joins moves into a short film, with a table of beats, the sound cues and the traps at each seam. `prompts/moves/RECIPES.md` has fifteen recipes: eight short films, six complete videos of 20 to 30 s, and one spectacle beat for any film. See [Recipes](/docs/recipes).

Pick the recipe from the family of your direction (type-led, object- or product-led, graphic- or colour-led). Then change at least two moves. A film that follows a recipe almost 1:1 gets advice from `dev`.

## Render a clip again

The clips are not tracked as masters. The web copy of each clip is `site/public/moves/<move>.mp4`. To render the master of one move, run this in a vawe checkout.

```bash
bin/vawe moves --only weight-morph
```

The verb renders the clip to `prompts/moves/<move>.mp4` at 1280 px and 60 fps, and writes a 12-frame contact sheet to `out/moves/<move>-sheet.png`. Without `--only`, it renders the clips whose demo, `demo/demo.css` or imported `core/` files changed. `--all` renders every clip.

## Limits

- A move is a snippet. It does not check your page. The checks of `dev` do that.
- A move earns its place with a real reference, an approved clip and a clear use. A clip that is not approved goes out of the library.
- Some moves are "once per film": `logo-sting`, `wordmark-cascade`, `mark-trace`, `shape-trace-morph`, `calm-lockup`, `thanks-sweep`, `chain-beats`, `rack-focus` and `skeleton-reveal`. Each move file says so.
