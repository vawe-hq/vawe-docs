This page lists the video limits of six platforms. Every row names the platform page that states the limit and the date the page was read. Where a platform publishes no number, the cell says "not published". A blog figure never fills a gap.

Platform limits change. Read the platform page again before a launch. The guides for each platform tell you the vawe commands: [YouTube](/docs/youtube), [YouTube Shorts](/docs/youtube-shorts), [Instagram Reels](/docs/instagram-reels), [TikTok](/docs/tiktok) and [LinkedIn](/docs/linkedin-video). A film for X uses the same commands as the others; the X row below gives its limits.

Notes on the rows:

- **YouTube Shorts.** The page "three-minute Shorts" states that a video with a square or vertical aspect ratio, up to three minutes long, is a Short, for uploads from 2024-10-15 (2025-12-08 for Official Artist Channels). The older Shorts upload page says only "vertical".
- **Instagram Reels, post.** The Instagram help page for Reel sizes (`help.instagram.com/1038071743007909`) did not load text on 2026-10-31. A search excerpt of that page gives a ratio range of 1.91:1 to 9:16, at least 30 fps and at least 720 px. Read the page before you rely on those numbers. The two pages in the table disagree on length: the feature page says 20 minutes of recording, and the creators FAQ says 3 minutes or less for recommendation.
- **TikTok, post.** `support.tiktok.com` answered 503 to a direct fetch on 2026-10-31. The numbers in the row come from a search excerpt of the English creator-tools page. A Latvian version of a TikTok page gives 10 minutes. Check your own locale.
- **LinkedIn, post.** The two LinkedIn help pages disagree. The video-sharing page gives 15 minutes and 5 GB. The Page video page gives 10 minutes. Use 10 minutes if you post from a Page.
- **Loudness.** No platform page above states a LUFS target for an upload. Do not copy a number from a blog. [Loudness and the mix](/docs/loudness-and-the-mix) shows how to measure and set the level of a film.
- **Frame rate in vawe.** `bin/vawe ship` renders at 60 fps and has no fps flag. A 60 fps film fits every row except the LinkedIn ad, which asks for less than 30 fps. See the [LinkedIn guide](/docs/linkedin-video).

## The vawe settings

These values come from `bin/vawe` and `core/layout/aspects.js`. Read [Aspect ratios](/docs/aspect-ratios) for the layout rules.

| Film | Aspect flag | Final size |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube, X, wide LinkedIn | `--aspect 16:9` | 1920 x 1080 |
| Shorts, Reels, TikTok, tall LinkedIn | `--aspect 9:16` | 1080 x 1920 |
| Square feed post | `--aspect 1:1` | 1080 x 1080 |
| LinkedIn feed post | `--aspect 4:5` | 1080 x 1350 |

Every final is H.264 High profile, `yuv420p`, with the moov atom at the front of the file (`+faststart`) and AAC audio at 48 kHz. Ask `bin/vawe ship --help` for the flags. `ship` also writes a small `.web.mp4` beside the master: do not upload that copy.
